How it works

The table. Built from every regular-season game in the save: two points for a win, one for an overtime loss. Teams level on points are split by fewer games played, then regulation wins, wins and goal difference, the NHL's order; the game's own order isn't confirmed.

Under the hood. Shot share is a team's part of all shot attempts in its games, the best quick read of who controls play. Sh% and Sv% are goals per shot, for and against; PDO adds them up, and a team far above or below 100 is usually riding luck that fades. Faceoffs is the share won.

The race. Each team gets a strength, in goals per game against an average team. It starts from the lineup: the average overall of the 12 forwards, the six defencemen and the starting goalie, weighted by how much each has mattered to goal difference in this league. Results then pull it toward the team's actual goal difference, slowly: a full season of games moves it about halfway. Every game left is played out times, with a small edge for the home team and % of games going to overtime for the loser's point.

Playoffs and the pick. Playoffs is how often a team finishes in the top three of its division or takes one of its conference's two wild cards. 1st pick is its chance at first overall once the lottery is run among the 16 teams that miss: the league's odds, with a winner able to move up at most ten spots.

How good is it? Checked against every game of 2026-27 and the start of this season, the lineup number called winners better than the standings did. It doesn't know about trades, injuries or call-ups that haven't happened, so read it as where things point today.
