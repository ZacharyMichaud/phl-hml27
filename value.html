How the grades work

What it measures. The change in a team's in-game value over each stretch of the league. Value is the game's own trade value, the number behind the trade screen, for every player whose rights a team holds: roster, minors, prospects and unsigned picks. Value given away by keeping salary in a trade is taken off.

The curve. Each stretch is graded against the other 31 teams, by how far a team's change sits from the league average. The average earns a B−; an A+ takes 1.6 standard deviations above it, and more than 1.6 below is an F. A stretch still running is marked so far, and its grades will move.

Moves and development. Moves are what a GM did: trades, the draft, free agency, re-signings and waivers. Development is what the players did: ratings going up or down, and the yearly rollovers. "Moves only" grades the first without the second.

Picks. Pick value isn't graded by itself. A pick counts when it is traded, at its value that day, or used: the player drafted is set against the value of the slot he was taken in. A pick that gains value because its team sinks in the standings earns nothing.

Where things land. Trades are valued just before they happen, without the 5% the trade screen adds to one side; what a player gains or loses after a trade is development for his new team. Contracts lose a year at the draft, which counts with the season rather than the draft. The summer rollover ages everyone a year and sits in the offseason.

Estimates. The game stores a player's value only when someone looks at him, so most saves hold few. A value comes from the save when it is stored there; otherwise it is copied from another save where the same player had the same rating, potential, contract and season; otherwise it is estimated from his own earlier value or from similar players.
